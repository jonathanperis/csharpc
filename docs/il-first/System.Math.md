Security: rounding with digits and `MidpointRounding`, `ScaleB`, `ILogB`, the saturating `ConvertToInteger` and the generic-math statics are CoreLib's own, with its argument checks (`ArithmeticException` for `Math.Sign(NaN)`, `ArgumentOutOfRangeException` for more than 15 digits (6 for float), `ArgumentException` for an undefined `MidpointRounding`) and its NaN and infinity handling. The libm-backed members (`Sin`, `Pow`, `Sqrt`...) stay csharpc's intrinsics. JIT intrinsics whose IL only calls itself (`ReciprocalEstimate`, `ReciprocalSqrtEstimate`, `MultiplyAddEstimate`, `SinCos`; `ConvertToIntegerNative` for a primitive target) are lowered by the compiler (`Intrinsics.cs`, `FunctionCodeGen.NativeConversions.cs`: `fdiv 1/x`, `1/llvm.sqrt`, `llvm.fma`, libm sin/cos, the x64/arm64 native conversion; on other targets an in-range value converts and NaN or an out-of-range one is an explicit "not supported by csharpc yet"), and the slicer refuses to copy any body that calls itself unconditionally, which would compile to an endless loop.
